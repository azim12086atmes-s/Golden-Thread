# Attire research — girl & boy wardrobe, modern to ancient

Research run 2026-09-25 for the wardrobe in `src/characters/outfits.ts`. It extends
[ART_BIBLE.md §2](../ART_BIBLE.md#2-wardrobe-notes) and follows its rule: **a reference that is not
covering is merged, not dropped** — full sleeves to the wrist, trousers to the ankle, a high or
modest neckline, a loose cut, and a head covering on the girl (`modestify()` in
`src/characters/modesty.ts`). Characters have no faces, so every look below is described only by
silhouette, layering, colour, trim, pattern and headwear.

**How to read a look:** *name · era · silhouette & layers · colours · trim/pattern · headwear ·
merge note* (merge note only where the reference itself is not covering).

**Sourcing.** Pinterest search and "ideas" pages were tried with a headless browser (text only,
images blocked, no login): both are now behind a sign-in wall and expose only the page title and
search count, so — as in the Art Bible — Pinterest board/idea titles were used for vocabulary and
garment structure was confirmed against museum, encyclopaedia and heritage-organisation pages (see
**Sources**). Everything here is summarised in our own words; colours are our palette choices
informed by the references, not sampled from images.

**Two cultural-care notes.**
- *Sámi gákti* is a living identity garment: the cut, collar and band sequence tell region, family
  and marital status. Our Arctic looks are **gákti-inspired** with invented band sequences — do not
  replicate a specific community's pattern, and label them "Arctic", not "Sámi".
- *Palestinian tatreez* motifs are regional signatures. Use generic geometric cross-stitch blocks,
  not a named village's motif set.
